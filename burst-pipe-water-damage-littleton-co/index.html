The first five minutes of burst pipe water damage

Burst pipe water damage gets worse by the minute, and most of the damage comes from what you do in the first few. Your goals are simple: stop the water, stay away from electricity, and get the right people moving. Everything else can wait.

A pipe that froze and split may not leak until it thaws, which is why a dripping ceiling can show up hours after the cold snap passed.

Shut off the water at the main

Find the main shut-off valve. Denver Water's cold weather tips say that in most single-family homes it sits in the basement or crawl space, on a wall that faces the street. Denver Water supplies Littleton residents, so those tips apply to local homes.

Turn the valve fully closed, then open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain what remains in the lines. If you cannot find the valve or it will not turn, call and tell the dispatcher so the next step can be planned.

Power and water do not mix

If water is near outlets, a panel, or running through a light fixture, stay out of the wet area. Switch off power only from a dry spot using a dry hand, and only if the panel is not standing in water. If you are unsure, leave the room and wait for a professional.

Thawing a frozen pipe the safe way

If a pipe is frozen but has not split, shut off the water first. Denver Water advises this before any thawing, because a pipe that cracked while frozen will flow once it warms. Use a hair dryer on low, working from the faucet end toward the frozen section, and never use a torch or open flame.

A frozen indoor meter is its own risk. Denver Water notes that it can flood a basement, and replacing it is at the homeowner's expense, so keep the meter area warm and watch it closely in a hard freeze.

  1. Close the main shut-off valve
  2. Open the nearest faucet so melting ice has somewhere to go
  3. Apply gentle heat with a hair dryer, moving continuously
  4. Check the pipe for cracks as it warms, and shut the valve again if you see water
  5. Call a plumber for a split pipe and a water restoration crew for any wet materials

What to remove and what to leave

Move rugs, small furniture, electronics, books and boxes out of the wet area, if it is safe to do so. Lift furniture legs onto foil or plastic so wood does not stain the floor. Leave saturated drywall, carpet pad and built-ins for the crew to assess.

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water and do not walk through water that could be touching live electrical parts.

Document before anything gets thrown out

Take photos and short videos of the pipe, the water line on walls, and every damaged item before it moves. Keep a list of what you tossed with rough ages and prices paid. Save any plumber receipt or notes showing what broke.

This is the record your insurer will ask for, so more is better than less. Keep damaged material only if your insurer asks for it, and otherwise photograph it first.

Drying after the leak is fixed

Drying usually takes several days, and the goal is to bring materials back to normal moisture readings before anything is closed up. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of wetting, so speed matters, but so does patience at the end. A wall that feels dry can still read wet inside.
