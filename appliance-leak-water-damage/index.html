Appliance leak water damage comes from machines that have a water line, a drain line or both. In Littleton, CO homes that means washing machines, dishwashers, refrigerator ice-maker lines, and the connections under kitchen sinks and disposals. The appliance itself is rarely the cause. The rubber hose, the clamp or the plastic fitting behind it usually is.

These leaks differ from a burst pipe because they are small and slow. A hose that weeps a cupful a day can wet a subfloor for weeks, and the first sign is often a stain on the ceiling of the room below.

Where appliance water goes

Water follows gravity and the path of least resistance, which is rarely where you are looking. A washer on an upper floor can leak down a wall cavity and show up as a ceiling stain in the kitchen. A dishwasher drain leak can run under cabinets and soften the toe kick long before the floor in front of it gets wet.

Safety checks before you move any appliance

Stop the water at the appliance first. Every washer, dishwasher and ice-maker line should have its own small valve, usually behind or beside the unit. If you cannot find it or it is stuck, close the main shut-off, which in most Littleton single-family homes is in the basement or crawl space on a street-facing wall.

Then deal with electricity. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor are dry, and otherwise switch off the breaker for that circuit. Pulling a wet washer out by the cord is how people get shocked, so wait for an electrician if water reached the receptacle.

Heavy appliances and wet floors

A full washer is heavy and a wet floor is slick. Do not tilt or drag a unit across damp flooring alone, because a slip while holding hot water or a loaded drum is a bigger problem than the leak.

Sudden or gradual: the insurance line

Colorado Division of Insurance advisories say the insured must take steps to prevent further damage but should get the insurer's instructions before repairs. A hose that bursts at 2 a.m. is usually treated as sudden and accidental. A hose that seeped for months is often treated as gradual or maintenance related, and many policies exclude it.

That is why early photos, the date you first noticed the water and the failed part all matter. Keep the old hose or fitting, and ask your insurer to explain how it treats appliance leaks.

Prevention that costs very little

  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel hoses and check the date stamped on them.
  • Close the washer valves when you leave town for more than a few days.
  • Put a small water sensor behind the dishwasher and under the sink.

Littleton, CO: shut-offs, contractors and claims

Denver Water notes that in most single-family homes the main shut-off valve is in the basement or crawl space on a wall facing the street. If an appliance valve fails, that main valve is your backup, so mark it before a weekend away.

Colorado DORA's consumer page says not every occupation is licensed, and the City of Littleton requires a contractor license or registration certificate to pull permits along with proof of liability insurance. When a repair after the leak involves permitted plumbing or electrical work, ask the contractor for its Littleton license.

Colorado Division of Insurance advisories say to start a claim as soon as you can and that an insurer must provide a copy of your policy within 3 business days of a request. Ask for it early so you know how an appliance leak is classified before the adjuster arrives.
