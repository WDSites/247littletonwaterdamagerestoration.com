What to do when water is spreading in Englewood

Englewood has a long relationship with water in the wrong places. The city's own history records that Little Dry Creek flooded the central business district in the spring of 1913, and the Mile High Flood District calls the 1965 South Platte flood the worst flood of record for the Denver area. Those events were decades ago, but they shaped how the city handles drainage, and older lots still sit close to the creek corridors.

The practical takeaway is simple: if you live or work near a drainage corridor, know your shut-off valve, check your sump or window wells before storms, and do not wait to call when water appears.

Stop the source

Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is the cause. Learn where yours is before a leak, and ask your water utility if you are unsure.

Mind the power

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. If not, stay out and call your utility or 911.

Lift valuables

Move electronics, papers and small furniture off wet floors and onto dry surfaces.

Keep out of contamination

Keep people and pets away from sewer or flood water until it is removed. If you smell gas, leave and call 911.

How Englewood utilities shape the first call

That matters when the water you are dealing with comes from the street side. A main line backup is the city's problem to clear, and the cleanup inside your home is ours. Our process follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. For plumbing or electrical work during the rebuild, the Englewood Building Division handles permits through an online portal, and the city's general line is 303-762-2300.

Landmarks and what they say about local water risk

CityCenter Englewood sits on the former Cinderella City mall site, which was demolished in 1998 before the Civic Center opened in 2000 with the Englewood light rail station. Buildings of that scale tend to have long drain runs and big roofs, so a roof or drain failure at a commercial property can put a lot of water into a lot of floor area.

Swedish Medical Center traces its sanatorium origin to 1905, a reminder that Englewood has both very old buildings and recent ones. Older plumbing and original supply lines are a common source of slow leaks, and we check for both when we map moisture.
