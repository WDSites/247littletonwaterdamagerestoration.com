Frozen pipe water damage starts when water inside a supply line turns to ice and expands. The ice itself rarely floods anything. The split it leaves behind does, and the water usually shows up when the pipe warms and the ice plug lets go.

In Littleton, CO, that means a house can look fine through the coldest night and flood by mid-afternoon. If you are reading this with water running, shut off the main valve and call our 24/7 line.

Where pipes freeze in Littleton houses

The usual trouble spots are lines in exterior walls, runs through garages, pipes in crawl spaces and any room that gets no heat. A bathroom over a garage and a hose bib line that was never drained both fit that pattern. Frozen pipe bursts cluster in hard cold snaps, so our call volume is seasonal.

A pipe also freezes more easily when heat is turned down for a trip or the power goes out.

When a frozen pipe is an emergency

A pipe that is frozen but not yet burst is an urgent problem, though not yet a flood. A pipe that has split and is now thawing is a full emergency, because it can release water at household pressure into a wall or ceiling you cannot see. The longer it runs, the more drywall, insulation and flooring it reaches.

Call right away if you hear running water inside a wall, see water coming through a ceiling or find a wet floor under a cold room. Do not wait for the temperature to rise, since rising temperature is exactly what starts the flow.

Safety steps for the first hour

  1. Find the main shut-off valve. Denver Water notes that in most single-family homes it sits in the basement or crawl space, on a wall that faces the street.
  2. Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it standing on a dry surface.
  3. Call our 24/7 line, then your insurer, and take photos before anything is moved.

If a pipe is frozen but not yet burst

Shut off the water first, then open the nearest tap so melting ice has somewhere to go. Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, working from the faucet end toward the frozen section. Never use a torch or open flame, which can scorch framing and split the pipe faster.

What changes the cost of a frozen pipe loss

  • How long the pipe ran before the water was shut off.
  • Whether the water stayed in one room or traveled through walls and ceilings to floors below.
  • How much drywall and insulation must be removed to reach wet framing.
  • The number of days equipment runs before readings reach target.

Insurance and burst pipes

The Colorado Division of Insurance advises that burst pipes and the resulting water damage are generally covered under homeowners and renters policies. The insured must take steps to prevent further damage, which is why shutting off the water matters, but should get the insurer's instructions before repairs.

Gradual leaks and long-term neglect are treated differently from a sudden burst. Read your policy, ask your insurer about deadlines, and keep your photos.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

  • Do not leave a frozen pipe alone and hope it holds. Ice that melts under pressure is how floods start.
  • Do not cover a wet wall before it is measured dry.
