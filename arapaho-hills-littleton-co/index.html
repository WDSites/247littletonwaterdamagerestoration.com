Arapaho Hills is a residential neighborhood in Littleton, Colorado. It was platted in 1955 and its historic district was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2012. The houses were designed in post-war modernist styles, so a leak here often reaches original wood, plaster and built-in finishes that owners want to keep.

Where Arapaho Hills sits

A City of Littleton historic survey says the subdivision began in unincorporated Arapahoe County and was later annexed to the city. It describes the platted area as lying between South Lowell Boulevard and South Camargo Road, and between West Berry Avenue and West Arrowhead Road. The city's landmarks page lists slightly different bounding streets, so we treat the edges as approximate and confirm coverage by street address.

The survey counts 83 lots in the original plat. The city dates the historic district to 1940 to 1965.

What the survey says about the houses

The survey places each house on a continuum between the International and Usonian styles, and says regional architect Bruce Sutherland designed most of them. Homes in these styles are generally known for large glass areas and exposed wood, so one wet wall can touch finishes that are hard to match.

The survey also notes that some houses show cast-in-place concrete foundations where the lot grade drops, and that some multi-level houses sit partly below grade. We do not assume any home has a basement. On the first call we ask which levels are below grade and what the lower floor is made of, because that changes the drying plan.

Historic homes and repairs

If your property is a city landmark or sits in a historic district, ask the City of Littleton's historic preservation staff whether repairs that change original features need review before work begins. Photograph original trim, flooring and built-ins before anything is removed.

Not every material can be saved. Moisture readings decide what dries in place and what has to come out.

First steps when water appears

Stop the source

Close the fixture shut-off valve or the main valve if you can reach it safely.

Mind the electricity

Do not touch the breaker panel while standing in water. If outlets or the panel are wet, stay out and call your utility or 911.

Photograph first

Photograph the damage and original finishes before anything is moved.

Lift small items

Move rugs, electronics and art onto dry surfaces.

Avoid contaminated water

Do not enter floodwater or sewage, and do not run household fans or a vacuum on sewage.

Call us

Describe the source, the rooms and the floor type. We explain what comes next.
