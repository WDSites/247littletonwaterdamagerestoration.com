Commercial water damage restoration covers losses in office, retail and shared-use buildings in Littleton, CO. The core work matches a home loss: stop the spread, extract water, remove what cannot be saved and dry the structure. The differences are scale, access and paperwork.

A broken supply line over a ceiling can wet several rooms, and a sprinkler or fixture failure can reach neighboring units. This page lays out what we can do and what the property owner or manager still has to handle.

How a commercial loss differs from a home

  • Footprints are larger, with more rooms and long runs of flooring.
  • Ceiling tiles and grid systems absorb water and sag, then drop.
  • Commercial carpet is often glued down over a concrete slab.
  • Concrete slab floors hold moisture longer than wood framing.
  • Shared walls and units mean water can move into spaces you do not control.
  • Keys, alarms and building access must be arranged before crews can start.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Find and close the water shut-off for the affected line or the building main, if you can reach it safely.
  2. Keep people out of wet areas. Slippery floors and sagging ceiling tiles are hazards.
  3. Do not touch outlets, panels or equipment that are wet. Have the building electrician or utility make them safe.
  4. Contact the building manager or owner, and the utility if the source is outside your control.
  5. Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved.
  6. Call our 24/7 line with the address, the source and who can give us access.

What the property owner or manager still handles

We do not communicate with tenants or customers for you. Notices, schedules and temporary relocation are the owner's or manager's job. We can tell you which areas are affected and for how long, in plain terms, so you can plan.

Building management also coordinates the utilities, fire alarm and sprinkler systems, and any landlord approvals. We do not repair plumbing, roofs or building systems, so a plumber or roofer handles those.

What changes the cost of a commercial loss

  • Total area wet and the number of rooms or units reached.
  • Flooring type, such as glued carpet, tile or sealed concrete.
  • Ceiling and wall materials that must be removed.
  • Days of drying and the number of machines needed.
  • Access limits, such as after-hours entry or restricted areas.
  • Water type, since contaminated water adds containment.

We explain what the job involves and what it costs before work starts, and we separate emergency steps from later repair items.

Insurance and documentation

Commercial property policies vary widely, so read yours rather than assume. Sudden and accidental water losses are often covered, while gradual leaks and flood often are not. Business interruption, if you carry it, is a separate part of a policy, and your agent can explain how it applies.

The Colorado Division of Insurance advises starting a claim as soon as possible, taking steps to prevent further damage and getting your insurer's instructions before repairs. We provide photos, moisture logs and a written scope that you can pass to your insurer.

Photograph first, then dry

Take wide and close photos of the source, the wet materials and the contents before anything is moved. It makes your insurance file easier to support.

Mistakes to avoid after a commercial water loss

  • Waiting until Monday because it happened on a weekend.
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles and carpet in place to see if they dry.
  • Letting repairs start before the structure has dried.

Littleton, CO: permits and contractors

The City of Littleton Permit Center in the Littleton Center, on Berry Avenue, requires permits for work on electrical, gas, mechanical or plumbing systems. A building owner whose rebuild reaches those systems should call 303-795-3751 before anyone starts.

The city requires a contractor license or registration certificate to pull permits, and it requires proof of liability insurance. Ask each contractor on your repair for its Littleton license and proof of insurance, since Colorado DORA notes that not every occupation, including general contractors, is licensed at the state level.
