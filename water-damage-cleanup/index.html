Water damage cleanup is the stage after the water is stopped, when ruined materials leave, surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, and odor is dealt with. The job in Littleton, CO often begins the same day as extraction, and the first 48 hours matter most. Wet carpet pad, insulation and porous drywall hold moisture and bacteria that grow quickly if they are left in place.

If you have just found damage, call the 24/7 line. Keep out of areas with sewage or unknown water, wear gloves if you must handle wet items, and do not run a household vacuum on wet floors.

The first 48 hours after water damage

Moisture, warmth and organic material give microbes what they need, and the clock starts when materials get wet. In the first day or two, the choices are whether to dry an item or to discard it, whether a surface can be cleaned in place, and whether the water carried contamination.

A quick cleanup visit decides those questions from evidence, not guesses. Moisture readings show what is still wet, and the water category sets the cleaning method for everything the water touched.

Sewage backup: what to do first

Sewage is Category 3 water and carries bacteria that cleaning wipes cannot handle. Stay out of it, keep children and pets away, and do not touch it with bare hands. If anyone in the home has a health concern after exposure, talk to a physician.

The City of Littleton handles sewer emergencies and maintenance, with a weekday line at 303-795-3959 and an after-hours line at 303-794-1551. Some addresses sit in sanitation districts that the city bills for sewer, so ask which one serves yours. Call the city for the blockage and call us for the contaminated cleanup.

Do not clean sewage yourself

A bucket and bleach will not make sewage-soaked drywall, pad or carpet safe. Porous materials soaked in contaminated water are removed, then hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized. This follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 practices that the trade publishes for water damage work, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

Odor is a symptom of something still wet

A musty smell a few days after water damage usually means moisture is still trapped in a material. Masking it with sprays only hides the sign. The fix is to find the wet material with a meter or thermal camera, remove or dry it, and then treat the air.

If the smell returns weeks later, check for a slow leak or for water that was missed behind baseboards and under cabinets. Left alone, those spots may call for mold remediation.

Documenting for your insurer

The Colorado Division of Insurance advises homeowners to contact their insurer as soon as possible and to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, while getting the insurer's instructions before repairs. It also notes that an insurer must supply a copy of your policy within three business days of a request. Ask for that copy before you decide what to throw away.

Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer about deadlines. We photograph conditions and list removed materials so your claim has a record.
