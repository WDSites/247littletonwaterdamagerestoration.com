Water mitigation is the work that happens in the first hours after a leak, flood or overflow, before anyone talks about rebuilding. It means stopping the source, extracting standing water, and setting drying equipment so wet materials return to normal moisture levels. In Littleton, CO, this is the window where a repair that stays small separates from one that grows into mold and rotted framing.

If water is moving through your home right now, call our 24/7 line at once. While you wait for a crew, shut off the main water valve, keep people and pets out of the wet area, and lift small valuables off the floor. Our dispatch team can talk you through the next few minutes.

When standing water is an emergency

Treat water as an emergency when it is still coming from a pipe, when it has reached carpet, drywall or insulation, or when it touches outlets and appliances. Porous materials such as drywall and carpet pad wick moisture upward and sideways, so a puddle at the baseboard can become a wet wall within hours.

Never step into water that could be energized. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground. If you are unsure, call the utility or 911 first and call us second.

Electrical and sewage safety

If water is near outlets, an electrical panel or appliances, stay out of the room. Call 911 or your utility for any electrical or gas concern before you call the restoration line. If the water came from a sewer line, treat it as contaminated and keep everyone out of it.

Structural drying: what the equipment actually does

Structural drying is the part of mitigation that removes moisture trapped inside wall cavities, subfloors and framing. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, and dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air so it does not settle back into the materials. Low grain refrigerant dehumidifiers work well in warm rooms, while desiccant units are chosen for cooler spaces such as many basements.

Drying is measured, not guessed. A technician records moisture content in drywall, wood and concrete at the start, then checks again each visit. Equipment stays until the readings match dry reference points for that material, which commonly takes several days instead of hours. This monitored approach follows the practices in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Opening a wall is sometimes the faster path. A small flood cut, usually about two feet up from the floor, lets air reach wet insulation that cannot be dried in place. The call to cut or dry in place is made from readings, and it is explained to you before it happens.

Why Littleton homes need quick mitigation

Denver Water supplies Littleton residents, and its cold weather guidance says the main shut-off in most single-family homes sits in the basement or crawl space on a wall facing the street. Knowing where yours is before a leak starts saves minutes when they matter, so find it now and make sure everyone in the house can turn it.

Winter adds another path for water. The National Weather Service reports that western and southwestern Denver suburbs average about 65 to 70 inches of snow per season, and melt from a heavy roof load can find its way behind ceilings. A ceiling stain after a thaw is a mitigation call, not a paint job.

What affects the cost of mitigation

Every job is priced from its own scope, and we explain the scope and price before work starts. These factors move the number most:

  • Water category: clean supply water, gray water from appliances, or contaminated black water.
  • Affected square footage and how many rooms and floors the water reached.
  • Materials involved, such as hardwood, carpet, plaster or concrete slab.
  • Equipment count and the number of drying days needed to reach target readings.
  • How much has to be removed and rebuilt once drying is complete.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Where mitigation ends and repair begins

Mitigation stops at dry. Once readings are at target and any contaminated material is out, the rebuild of drywall, flooring and paint is a separate phase covered under water damage repair. If the water was sewage or came from outside the building, the cleanup stage is described on our water damage cleanup page.
