About 24/7 Littleton Water Damage Restoration

A Littleton-based dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation.

Call (303) 952-8295

24/7 Littleton Water Damage Restoration is a 24/7 dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation in Littleton, Colorado, and the towns around it. Our address is 709 W Littleton Blvd, Littleton, CO 80120. When you call, you reach a dispatcher who asks what is wet, where the water came from and whether it is still running.

How the service works

Restoration is delivered by licensed and insured professionals. We explain the scope of work and the price before work starts, and the work is documented with photos and moisture readings. We do not sell products, rent equipment or run forms. Everything starts with a phone call.

What we follow and what we do not claim

We follow ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration and S520 for mold remediation as published industry standards, and you are welcome to ask any company, including ours, whether its crews work to them. We do not publish reviews, ratings or job counts, because we only state what we can show.

We are not a waterproofing company, and we do not repair foundations. If your basement keeps taking on water, we will say so and point you to the right trade.

What happens when you call

A dispatcher asks four things: where you are, what is wet, where the water came from and whether it is still running. If the source is still active, you get plain instructions first, such as closing the main valve or staying out of a room with an electrical risk.

Until the crew arrives, here is what to do: close the main valve if a pipe is the source, stay out of water near outlets and call 911 for any electrical or gas risk. Next, a crew is arranged and the visit starts with a moisture check. The scope of work and the price are explained before any equipment is set or any material is removed, and you can ask questions at that point.

Why the work is split into stages

Water damage is easier to understand and to document when it is divided into stages: stopping and removing the water, drying the structure, cleaning and sanitizing, and rebuilding. Each stage has its own scope, which helps when an insurer reads the file and when you compare what was done with what was planned.

Mold is treated as a related service. It usually follows moisture that stayed too long, so the same moisture mapping that guides drying also guides mold work.

Where we work

We work across Littleton and in Englewood, Denver, Lone Tree, Aurora and Morrison. The full list of water mitigation, cleanup and repair services is on the services page.

How we think about honest pricing

Restoration prices vary with the size of the wet area, the type of water, the materials involved and how long the water sat. Because of that, we do not publish a price list. Instead, the scope and the price are explained before work starts, and changes to the scope are discussed before they happen.

If a job needs a specialist outside water damage, such as a plumber for the source or a roofer for a leak that starts above, we say so. Fixing the source is part of fixing the damage, and drying a room while the leak continues only repeats the problem.

What we expect of the crews we send

  • Check moisture levels with a meter and record them.
  • Explain what is being removed and why before it is removed.
  • Set drying equipment only where readings show it is needed.
  • Keep photos and notes that you can share with your insurer.
  • Tell you plainly when something is outside the scope of restoration.

What counts as an emergency

Call right away when water is actively running, when more than a small area is wet, when the water may be sewage or flood water, or when water is near electrical equipment. Waiting lets water travel into wall cavities and under flooring, where it is harder to dry and where mold can start. A small, clean, one-time spill that you can fully mop and dry yourself usually does not need a crew.

If you are unsure, call and describe what you see. A dispatcher can tell you whether it sounds like something to handle yourself or something that needs equipment, and explain what the first visit would involve.

We would rather tell you a job is small than sell you a bigger one, and we would rather explain a limit than hide it. That is the standard we hold the line to.

Standards and sources we point to

For moisture and mold basics we point homeowners to the US EPA and to Colorado public health guidance, and for industry practice to ANSI/IICRC S500 and S520 as published standards. We link to these sources on the guide pages so you can read them yourself instead of taking our word for it.
