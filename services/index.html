How the stages fit together

If water is moving, call the 24/7 line and start with emergency water removal. Drying follows under water mitigation, then cleanup and repair. Floodwater and sewage take the stricter path described under flood damage restoration, and any growth that appears is handled under mold remediation.

Every service starts with the same promise: we explain the scope and the price before work starts. Insurance may cover sudden and accidental damage, and your insurer has the final word, so check your policy.

What to expect on the first visit

The first visit is a walk through the damage with a moisture meter and, where useful, a thermal camera. The crew looks for the source, marks what is wet and tells you which services apply and in what order. You hear the scope and the price before equipment is set or anything is removed.

If water is still active when you call, the dispatcher gives you instructions first: close the main valve, stay away from electrical hazards and keep people and pets out of contaminated water. Those steps protect your home and your family while a crew is on the way.

How to choose the right service

  • Water is still running or standing: start with emergency water removal. Stopping the source and extracting water comes before anything else.
  • Water is out but walls, floors or carpet are wet: water mitigation covers structural drying, moisture mapping and equipment.
  • Floodwater, creek overflow or sewage: flood damage restoration, because contaminated water needs removal of porous materials rather than drying them in place.
  • Everything is dry but damaged: cleanup first, then repair of drywall, insulation, flooring and paint.
  • Musty smell, visible growth or a leak that sat for days: mold remediation, with the moisture source fixed first.

Why speed matters in Colorado homes

Wet drywall and wood start to break down within days, and mold can begin on damp materials in roughly one to two days under typical indoor conditions, according to EPA mold guidance. Colorado's dry air helps drying once water is removed, but it does not rescue materials trapped behind baseboards, under flooring or inside wall cavities. That is why extraction and drying equipment are used even when the room feels dry to the touch.

Basements are the most common problem in Littleton and the surrounding towns. Finished basements hide wet carpet pad, insulation and the bottom of drywall, so a moisture meter is more reliable than looking and feeling. Ask the crew to show you the readings and the areas they marked.

Insurance and documentation

Policies differ on what counts as sudden and accidental damage, and flood coverage is often separate from standard homeowners coverage. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope make a claim easier to review. Keep receipts, take photos before anything is removed and call your insurer promptly. Your insurer makes the coverage decision.
