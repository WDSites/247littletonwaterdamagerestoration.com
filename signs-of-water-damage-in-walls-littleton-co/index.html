Signs of Water Damage in Walls in Littleton, CO

Walls hide a lot. Here is what the surface tells you, what the tools behind the surface tell a crew, and when a small clue is worth a phone call.

By the 24/7 Littleton Water Damage Restoration team. Updated 2026.

Why the signs of water damage in walls are easy to miss

Most wall leaks start inside the cavity, between the drywall and the studs. Supply lines, drain lines, window flashing and appliance hookups all run through that space, so a slow drip can wet insulation and paper for weeks before anything shows on the paint. By the time you see it, the damage is usually bigger than the spot suggests.

The signs of water damage in walls fall into a few groups: what you see, what you feel, what you smell, and what only a meter can read. One clue alone can be a false alarm. Two or three together deserve action.

Stains, bubbling paint and texture changes

Yellow or brown stains are the classic sign. They often start as a faint tea-colored patch and grow a ring at the edge as the wall dries and gets wet again. A stain with a clear ring has probably been wet more than once.

Bubbling or peeling paint means moisture is pushing outward from behind the surface. Drywall that feels soft, crumbly or swollen at a seam is holding water in the paper. Wallpaper that lifts at the seams and baseboard paint that cracks along the floor line point the same way.

  • Stains that grow after rain, showers or the dishwasher cycle
  • Paint that blisters, flakes or feels damp to the back of your hand
  • Drywall seams that ripple, bulge or show nail heads that have popped
  • Wall texture that looks smoothed out or sagging in one patch

Warm spots, cool spots and the musty smell

Wet drywall conducts heat differently than dry drywall. A patch that feels cooler than the wall around it can mean cold supply water or evaporation is pulling heat out. A warm patch, especially near a water heater or along a hot line, can point to a leaking hot supply pipe.

A musty or earthy smell is the other big clue. It tends to be strongest when the room is closed up, and it can come from a wall you cannot see any damage on. Treat the smell as a reason to look, not as proof of anything specific.

Soft baseboards, warped trim and sticking doors

Press gently on the baseboard near corners, under windows and beside bathrooms. Wood or MDF that gives under your thumb, swells at the bottom or separates from the wall has been absorbing water. Door casings that swell and doors that suddenly stick can show the same thing, because wood trim takes on moisture quickly.

Floors give hints too. Cupped laminate or hardwood next to a wall often means water is traveling from the wall into the floor system.

Pencil test

Trace the outer edge of any stain with a pencil and write the date beside it. If the stain grows past the line a day or two later, the source is active and the wall is still getting wet.

How moisture meters and thermal imaging find hidden leaks

A pin or pinless moisture meter reads how wet the drywall, wood or plaster is, and a crew compares those numbers to a dry reference spot in the same room. That comparison matters because every house has its own baseline. A reading is only useful next to a known dry reading.

Thermal imaging cameras show surface temperature differences. Wet material often looks cooler or warmer than dry material around it, which helps a technician decide where to take meter readings. The camera does not see water directly, so a good crew confirms with a meter before opening anything.

What a crew does once the leak is located

The first job is stopping the source. After that, wet insulation and drywall that cannot dry in place are typically cut out, and the cavity is opened enough for air to move through it. Drying equipment then runs for several days while readings are taken until the wall matches the dry reference.

That process is covered in our water mitigation work, and the rebuilding afterward falls under water damage repair. Crews following ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard, document readings so you can see the wall reaching dry. You can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

When it is time to call

Call right away if a stain keeps growing, a wall feels wet to the touch, or you can hear dripping inside a wall. Water damage often starts before mold, and the usual guidance is that mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of wetting.
