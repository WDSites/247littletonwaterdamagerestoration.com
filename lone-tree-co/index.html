Littleton and nearby towns

Lone Tree sits southeast of Littleton in Douglas County, about eight miles by C-470 and I-25. It is a relatively young city. Its voters approved incorporation in 1995, and it became a home rule city in 1998, according to its history. The Census counted 14,253 residents in 2020 and 15,278 in the 2025 estimate, spread over about ten square miles.

Water damage restoration in Lone Tree, CO is rarely about decades-old plumbing. It is about supply lines, appliance connections, water heaters and finished basements in newer houses, and the water they release spreads quickly.

How newer housing changes the water damage picture

Newer homes are built tighter. Tight construction keeps conditioned air in, but it also keeps moisture in, so a small leak behind a finished wall can raise humidity for weeks without any visible stain. We find it with a moisture meter and a thermal camera, then open only what we have to.

The finished lower level is the other pattern. A basement with carpet, drywall and built-ins can take a few hours of water and need a full strip-out, because the materials behind the finish hold moisture against the slab.

Where Lone Tree's water goes after a storm

The city is the primary stormwater manager under its permit, and parts of Lone Tree lie in the Cherry Creek Basin, draining toward Cherry Creek Reservoir. East of I-25, the Happy Canyon Creek Stream Improvements project is stabilizing channels for higher flows from denser development. Construction began in June 2023 in five phases, with the full project projected to finish in the fourth quarter of 2026.

None of that project changes what happens in your basement during a bad storm. If a window well fills or a sump pump stops, the water is yours to deal with, so test the pump before the season and keep the wells clear.

A seasonal first look: spring runoff to winter freezes

The metro area sees its heaviest snow loads in winter and spring. The National Weather Service says western and southwestern Denver suburbs average 65 to 70 inches per season, and while Lone Tree is southeast, the same freeze and thaw cycles apply to roofs, gutters and exposed pipes. A cracked line in a garage wall or an attic leak after a thaw are common winter calls.

Summer brings thunderstorms and the occasional hail event that opens a roof. Look for ceiling stains the day after a storm, not a month later.

What to do when a leak is spreading in Lone Tree

Shut the water off

Close the main valve if a supply line or water heater is leaking.

Power first

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground.

Save what you can

Move electronics, rugs and boxes off wet floors.

Stay clear of contamination

Keep children and pets away from sewer water. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911.

Landmarks, large buildings and water

Park Meadows, Lone Tree Arts Center, Sky Ridge Medical Center and the RidgeGate planned community are among the places that define the city. Large commercial and medical buildings have long drain runs and roof systems, and a single failure can put water through several floors. If you manage a building like that, call dispatch as soon as the leak is found and tell us which floors are affected.

For a house, the same speed applies. Our water mitigation crews set up extraction and drying equipment the same day when available.
