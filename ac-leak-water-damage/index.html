AC leak water damage happens when the water an air conditioner normally removes from your indoor air ends up somewhere else. Cooling a house wrings moisture out of the air, and that condensate is supposed to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs, the pan cracks or the coil ices over and thaws, the water goes into the ceiling, the closet or the floor.

In Littleton, CO the dry climate makes this less dramatic than in humid states. NWS Boulder puts the metro's normal annual precipitation at 14.48 inches, so a stain after a hot week is more likely to be condensate than rain. Many Front Range homes also use evaporative coolers, and central air leaks are a real but modest share of what we see.

Where AC leaks start in a house

The usual culprits are the condensate drain line, the drain pan under the air handler, and the evaporator coil. Algae and dust build a slime plug in the drain line, and the pan overflows. A coil that froze from low airflow or a dirty filter can thaw all at once and overwhelm the pan.

The location of the air handler matters most. In an attic or a second-floor closet, a small overflow lands on drywall and insulation. In a basement utility room, the same overflow reaches a concrete floor and does less harm.

Safety steps when an air handler leaks

  1. Turn the thermostat to off and switch off the breaker for the air handler. Running a unit with a clogged drain pushes more water out.
  2. Do not touch the unit or stand in water if the closet or attic floor is wet. Power and water together are a shock risk.
  3. Catch what you can with a bucket or towels below the drip, and place plastic on furniture beneath a stained ceiling.
  4. If the ceiling is bulging, keep people out of the room. A sagging board can fall and should be relieved by a trained crew.
  5. Call an HVAC contractor for the unit and call us for the water.

A bulging ceiling is a safety issue

Water trapped above a ceiling is heavy and can bring the board down. Stay out from under it, and do not poke it open yourself.

Insurance and the AC leak

Colorado Division of Insurance advisories say most property policies handle sudden and accidental water damage differently from long-term seepage. A condensate line that clogged and overflowed over a weekend may be treated as sudden, while a drip that ran all season may be treated as gradual. Ask your insurer how it classifies your case, and get its instructions before repairs.

Prevention before the cooling season

  • Have an HVAC contractor flush the condensate line before summer.
  • Change the filter on schedule so the coil does not freeze.
  • Add a float switch that shuts the unit off if the pan fills.
  • Do not ignore a small ceiling stain after the system runs.

Littleton, CO: climate, permits and contractors

NWS Boulder lists Denver's normal annual precipitation at 14.48 inches, so rain is a less common indoor water source in summer than a clogged AC drain. When you see a ceiling stain in July with no storms, check the air handler first.

The City of Littleton Permit Center requires a permit to install, alter, repair or replace any mechanical system. If your HVAC contractor replaces the unit or alters ducts, ask whether a permit is needed, and ask for the contractor's Littleton license.

Colorado DORA's consumer page says not all occupations are licensed. The city requires proof of liability insurance from contractors who pull permits, so confirm it before the work starts.
