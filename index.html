Water damage restoration in Littleton, CO: what it covers

Water damage restoration is the whole job from the first call to a finished room: stopping the water, removing it, drying the structure, cleaning what was touched and rebuilding what could not be saved. In Littleton, CO most calls begin with a burst or failed supply line, a water heater, a washing machine hose, a roof leak or a flooded basement. The sooner the water is out and the equipment is running, the more of the house can be saved.

We treat the work as stages with separate scopes. Water mitigation and emergency water removal come first, followed by water damage cleanup, and finally water damage repair for the rebuild. Splitting the stages keeps the plan clear and gives your insurer a record to read.

Emergency first: what to do before the crew arrives

Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is the source. Denver Water supplies Littleton residents, and its guidance says the main valve in most single-family homes is in the basement or crawl space, on a wall facing the street. Find yours today so you do not search for it in a flood.

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground, move small valuables up, photograph the damage and call. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 or your utility before you call us.

What a water damage job costs, and what moves the number

Callers often ask for a number before anything else, and the honest answer is that the number comes from the scope. We explain the scope and how it is priced before work starts, and we do not quote a flat figure over the phone for a loss we have not seen. These are the factors that move the price most:

Cost factors in water damage restoration
FactorWhy it matters
Water categoryClean, gray and contaminated water need different protection and removal.
Affected areaSquare footage and number of rooms set the equipment and labor.
Materials involvedHardwood, carpet, plaster and slab dry differently.
Drying daysMore days and more equipment add to the total.
Rebuild scopeDrywall, flooring, cabinets and paint after drying are priced separately.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and ask for a copy of the policy if you do not have one.

Documentation for your insurance claim

We photograph the damage, record moisture readings and list removed materials, so the claim has a record. The Colorado Division of Insurance advises homeowners to contact their insurer as soon as possible after a loss and to get the insurer's instructions before repairs. We do not approve claims or bill your insurer directly, and we never promise what a policy will pay.

Littleton: the water, the creeks and the places we work around

Littleton sits where the South Platte River leaves the foothills, with Big Dry Creek, Lee Gulch and Slaughterhouse Gulch crossing the city. The June 1965 South Platte flood reached the city at about 110,000 cubic feet per second, and Chatfield Dam was built afterward. The dam lowers river risk, but storm runoff and failed plumbing still send water into Littleton basements every year.

Around the city, Hudson Gardens on South Santa Fe Drive, the Littleton Museum on South Gallup Street and the Town Hall Arts Center on Main Street anchor the older core, and the Downtown Littleton Historic District was designated in 2021. Older buildings there often carry original plumbing and finishes, so a slow leak behind plaster or an old drain line is a regular find.

AdventHealth Littleton on South Broadway is the city's hospital, and the light rail line runs through downtown. Wherever you are, the first call is the same. Describe what is wet and where, and we explain what happens next.

Mold after water damage and what is not water damage restoration

Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours in wet porous materials, so quick drying is the best defense. When growth is already present, mold remediation covers containment, removal and clearance support, after the moisture source is fixed. We follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 and S520 standards for water damage and mold work as industry practice, and you can ask any company whether its crews do the same.

We are not a waterproofing or foundation company. If water keeps entering after a basement is restored, you need a drainage contractor, and we will tell you honestly what we saw.
