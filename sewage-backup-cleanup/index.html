A sewage backup happens when waste water cannot leave the house and comes back up through the lowest opening it finds. In Littleton, CO homes that is usually a basement floor drain, a shower, a tub or a toilet. The cleanup is a restoration job and a contamination job at the same time.

The usual causes are a blocked main line, tree roots in an older lateral, a collapsed section of pipe, or heavy rain that overloads the lines. Which one it is decides who you call first, and that is covered below.

When a sewage backup is an emergency

Treat every sewage backup as urgent. The water is classed as Category 3 (black water) in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water categories, which means it can carry bacteria and other contaminants. It also soaks into carpet, pad, drywall and insulation within hours.

Waiting turns a cleanup into a demolition. Porous materials that sit in sewage cannot be made clean again, so the sooner they come out, the less of the house is affected.

Safety steps for the first hour

  1. Stop using every toilet, sink, shower, dishwasher and washing machine.
  2. Do not use a household wet vacuum or mop on the water.
  3. Keep children, pets and anyone with a medical concern out of the affected rooms.
  4. Do not walk through the water. If you must cross it, wear waterproof boots and gloves, and wash well afterward.
  5. If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, stay away from them and have an electrician or your utility make it safe.
  6. Call the right party for the source, then call our 24/7 line for the cleanup.

Who to call first: the utility or a plumber

If every drain in the house is slow or backing up, the blockage is probably in the main line. In Littleton the city handles sewer emergencies and maintenance, at 303-795-3959 on weekdays from 8 to 4 and 303-794-1551 after hours. A main line backup is a utility call first.

If only one fixture is affected, the clog is likely in your own private line, and a plumber is the right trade. We clean up the contamination inside the house. We do not clear drains or repair sewer pipe.

How sewage backup cleanup works

1

Contain and protect

We set barriers over doorways and vents, and crews wear gloves, boots, respirators and protective suits.

2

Extract the water

Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water out of the space.

3

Remove porous materials

Carpet, pad, the lower section of drywall, insulation and soaked cabinets are cut out and bagged.

4

Clean and sanitize

Hard surfaces, framing and the slab are cleaned, then treated with an antimicrobial.

5

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture meters show the structure at dry standard.

Insurance and sewage backups

Sewer backup coverage is often an optional add-on to a homeowners policy, not part of the base policy. Read your declarations page or ask your agent whether you have it. The Colorado Division of Insurance advises starting a claim as soon as you can and getting your insurer's instructions before repairs.

Most property policies do not cover flooding. If the sewage came in because a creek or storm flooded the area, see flood damage restoration and ask your insurer how the loss is classified.

Mistakes to avoid after a backup

  • Keeping the soaked carpet because it looks fine once dry.
  • Using bleach on porous materials, which does not reach the contamination inside them.
  • Running a fan across the wet area, which moves contamination through the house.
  • Letting the room sit for days while waiting for the plumber.

Do not flush or snake your way out

Running water to see if the line clears makes a backup bigger. Wait for the utility or a plumber.
