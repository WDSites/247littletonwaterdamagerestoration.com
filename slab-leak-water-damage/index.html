A slab leak means a water supply or drain line is leaking inside or beneath a poured concrete slab. In Littleton, CO, this is the uncommon case. Most homes here have a basement or a crawl space, so the pipes run in the open overhead where a leak shows up fast.

Slab leaks do happen in slab-on-grade additions, attached garages, some ranch homes and in basements where lines were cast into or buried under the floor. If your leak is in a basement, our basement water damage page covers the common version, and general incidents are covered under water mitigation.

When a slab leak is an emergency

A hidden leak under concrete runs around the clock, and the water has nowhere to go but sideways into flooring, framing and the soil below. Treat it as urgent if you hear running water with every tap closed, see wet carpet on a slab floor or find the floor warm in one spot.

A pressurized line can saturate a room in a day. Call our 24/7 line and we will dispatch when a crew is available, and we will not promise an arrival time before we know the schedule.

First hour safety steps

  1. Find the main shut-off and close it. Most single-family homes have it in the basement or utility area.
  2. If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, keep clear and cut power only from a dry spot. Call 911 or your utility if you cannot do that safely.
  3. Photograph the floor and any meter reading before anything is moved.
  4. Call a plumber or leak-detection trade, then call us to start drying.

Do not break the slab yourself

Cutting into concrete can hit a live line, a gas pipe or wiring. Leave that to the trade that finds the leak.

How we dry a wet slab floor

Concrete is porous and holds water far longer than carpet or paint. We start by mapping moisture with meters and a thermal camera so we know how far the damp has traveled before we pull anything out.

1

Map the moisture

Meters and thermal imaging show the damp edge on floors, baseboards and the lower wall.

2

Remove what cannot dry

Soaked carpet pad, baseboard and the base of wet drywall come out so the slab can breathe.

3

Set drying equipment

Air movers push dry air across the surface while LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room.

4

Monitor daily

We recheck readings each visit and adjust the equipment until the slab and framing reach dry targets.

Insurance questions after a slab leak

Colorado Division of Insurance advisories say burst pipes and the resulting water damage are generally covered under homeowners and renters policies. They also tell policyholders to contact the insurer quickly and to get its instructions before repairs, while still taking steps to prevent more damage.

A leak that ran for months can be treated as gradual, and gradual damage is often not covered. Ask your insurer how it handles the pipe repair and the damage separately, and ask about deadlines.

Mistakes to avoid with a hidden leak

  • Ignoring a small rise in the water bill for several weeks.
  • Laying new flooring over a slab that has not been checked for moisture.
  • Drilling or cutting concrete to chase the leak.
  • Skipping photos of the floor and meter.

Littleton, CO: soil movement and permits

The Colorado Geological Survey notes that expansive clay soils lie under much of the plains where most Coloradans live, and they begin to swell once they get wet. Poor drainage is a common problem. Cracked floor slabs are among the common signs of soil movement, so a cracked slab near wet flooring is worth showing to your plumber.

The City of Littleton Permit Center, at 303-795-3751, requires a permit to install, alter, repair or replace any plumbing system. If the repair involves cutting into the slab and rerouting a line, ask the Permit Center whether your case needs one before the plumber starts.

Census QuickFacts puts the owner-occupied rate at 61.2 percent. If you rent, tell your landlord first, since the line repair and claim may be theirs.
