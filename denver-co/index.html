Water damage restoration in Denver, CO from our Littleton base is mostly about what those blocks are made of. Brick ranches, bungalows and mid-century homes have basements, older supply lines and finishes that tell us a lot about where water went.

What to do if water is still spreading in southwest Denver

Close the main

Find the shut-off, usually where the water line enters the house, and turn it fully off.

Check the power

If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call your utility or 911 before calling us.

Protect the floor

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors. Photograph the damage first.

Avoid sewage

If the water smells like sewage, stay out and keep children and pets away. If you smell gas, leave and call 911.

Floods in Denver's history and what they mean for a basement

The Mile High Flood District calls the 1965 South Platte flood Denver's worst flood of record, with about 300 million dollars in losses in the city, and the district itself was formed in 1969 in response. The USGS notes that the South Platte at Denver peaked at 3,930 cubic feet per second on September 12, 2013, compared with 40,300 in 1965.

Cherry Creek has its own record: a flood in 1864, and the failure of Castlewood Dam in 1933. The Cherry Creek Dam, built in the early 1950s, is credited with preventing about 130 million dollars in damage in 1965. A dam lowers the risk, and it does not stop a burst pipe or a clogged window well.

Practically, a Denver basement on a lower elevation can see water from a storm, a water heater, or a failed sump pump. If you are near the river or a gulch, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer what is covered.

Utilities and permits in Denver
