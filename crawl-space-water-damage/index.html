Crawl space water damage is water sitting in the shallow space between the soil and the floor of your house. In Littleton, CO, crawl spaces are less common than basements. They turn up mainly in older homes and in some additions built onto them.

That makes it easy to miss a problem for weeks, because nobody goes down there. Water in a crawl space does not stay there, since it works up into the floor joists, subfloor and the rooms above.

When water under the house is an emergency

Standing water that touches ducts, wiring or insulation is urgent. Wet wood framing can start to host mold in 24 to 48 hours, and a damp space rarely dries without help because it has little airflow.

Call right away if you see water through the access hatch, notice a sour smell inside, or feel soft spots in the floor above. We dispatch around the clock when a crew is available and will not promise an arrival time.

Crawl space safety before anyone goes in

A crawl space is a confined space with wiring, ducts and sometimes standing water. Do not crawl into water while power is on to anything beneath the house. Turn off power from the panel only if you can reach it on dry ground.

  • Close the main water shut-off if a pipe is the source.
  • Keep children and pets away from the hatch.
  • Never enter alone, and never enter if you smell gas or see sparking.
  • Take photos from the opening before anything is disturbed.
  • Call us and the plumber or utility as needed.

What we do not do

We do not install vapor barriers or encapsulation, and we do not regrade or handle drainage. If water keeps coming in, a drainage or waterproofing contractor fixes the cause while we deal with the damage.

How we pump and dry a crawl space

1

Pump out standing water

Submersible pumps and extraction units remove the water, and we log where it entered.

2

Remove wet insulation

Soaked batts hold water against joists and sag, so they come out first.

3

Dry the wood

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until joists and subfloor meet the readings in the ANSI/IICRC S500 drying approach.

4

Check and monitor

Moisture meters and a thermal camera track progress on each visit.

What drives the cost of crawl space work

  • Depth and volume of standing water.
  • Access size, since a tight hatch slows equipment and crews.
  • How much insulation and ducting is wet.
  • How long the wood has been damp.

Before any work starts, we explain what the job involves and what it will cost, so there are no surprises once we are under the house.

Insurance and mistakes to avoid

Colorado Division of Insurance advisories say most property policies do not cover flooding, while burst pipes and the resulting water damage are generally covered. Start a claim early, take steps to prevent more damage and get the insurer's instructions before repairs.

Common mistakes: leaving wet insulation in place, running a household fan down the hatch and hoping, sealing the space before it dries, or waiting weeks because the crawl space is out of sight. Ask your insurer about deadlines.
