Basement water damage is the cleanup of a below-grade space after water gets in. In Littleton, CO, the basement is the most common place for water, because it sits lowest, touches the soil and holds the water heater, the sump and many plumbing runs.

Water enters through window wells, foundation cracks, failed sump pumps, plumbing breaks, sewer backups and groundwater after snowmelt. We clean up and dry the space. We do not waterproof, so a drainage or waterproofing contractor handles the entry point.

Start by naming the water

Water type decides how much comes out. A broken supply line gives clean water that many materials survive if you act fast. Groundwater and storm water carry soil and bacteria, which pushes the job toward flood damage restoration.

Sewage is the most serious case. If a floor drain is backing up, read sewage backup cleanup and keep everyone out of the space.

Basement flooding safety in the first hour

  1. Stay out of standing water until power to the area is off. Cut it from the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  2. Close the main water valve if a pipe or heater is the source.
  3. Keep children and pets out of the space.
  4. Move anything valuable off the floor to a dry spot, but do not carry wet furniture through the house.
  5. Call us and your insurer, and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

The 24 to 48 hour window

Mold can begin on damp materials in roughly 24 to 48 hours. Pulling wet carpet pad and drying the slab early is the best way to keep a wet basement from turning into a mold job.

How we clean up a wet basement

1

Extract the water

Truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove standing water from the slab and carpet.

2

Remove the unsalvageable

Carpet pad, baseboards, insulation and the bottom two feet of wet drywall usually go first.

3

Clean and sanitize

The slab and framing are cleaned, and antimicrobial treatment is used where the water type calls for it.

4

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture meters and thermal cameras confirm progress.

Drying below grade is slow because concrete and soil keep releasing moisture. A finished basement has more to remove and a longer path to dry than a bare one, and stored boxes on the floor should be sorted early. Cardboard soaks fast and holds odor.

Insurance and prevention

FEMA's NFIP site notes a typical 30 day wait before a new flood policy takes effect, and most homeowners policies do not cover flood. A burst pipe is a different case and is generally covered, so tell your insurer how the water got in.

Prevention starts outside. Keep gutters clear, point downspouts away from the foundation, check the sump pump before spring melt and test the battery backup. Fixing the grading is a drainage trade's job.

Littleton, CO: floodplains, soils and sewers

Littleton has FEMA and local floodplains, and work inside one needs a Floodplain Development Permit. The city's floodplain administrator can be reached at 303-795-3863, so call before you hire anyone to dig or regrade near a creek.

The Colorado Geological Survey says cracked basement floor slabs are among the common signs of soil movement, and expansive clay swells when wet. A new crack in the slab is worth showing to a foundation specialist.

For sewer backups, the City of Littleton handles emergencies at 303-795-3959 on weekdays and 303-794-1551 after hours. Confirm which district serves your address, since some areas are served by other sanitation districts.
